Frequently Asked Questions

Quick answers to standard questions about our programs and wicking beds.

Traditional garden beds lose up to 60% of applied water to direct sun evaporation and deep drainage runoff. Wicking beds store water in an enclosed bottom basin beneath a geotextile barrier. Moisture is drawn upward naturally via soil capillary tension only when plant roots need it, reducing water usage by half and virtually eliminating weeds.

Schools in Western Kenya can submit an inquiry via our contact form or contact our Kisumu office. We conduct a preliminary site inspection (evaluating sunlight, rainwater runoff, and student club capacity), provide container hardware and seedling kits, and train teaching staff on integrating the beds into the Competency-Based Curriculum (CBC).

We publish annual impact and financial reports with verified independent audits. Donors who sponsor school gardens receive geotagged photographic progress reports, pupil harvest updates, and direct contact with school leadership.
